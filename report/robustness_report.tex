\section*{Target article}
\bibentry{luo2020}

\section{Goal}
\label{sec:goal}

Luo et al.\ concluded from 14 randomised trials that electronically delivered cognitive behavioural therapy (eCBT) is more effective for depression than face-to-face CBT: standardised mean difference $\pubOrig$ [95\% CI $\pubOrigLb$, $\pubOrigUb$] \cite{luo2020}. Three documents followed. An addendum reinterpreted the same estimate as less improvement with eCBT \cite{luo2020addendum}. A corrigendum revised it to $\pubCorr$ and stated that the conclusions were unchanged \cite{sanger2021corrigendum}. A letter of concern showed that standard errors (SEs) had been entered as standard deviations (SDs); with SDs, it found no significant difference, $\krPub$ [$\krPubLb$, $\krPubUb$] \cite{kriston2022concerns}. The article's abstract still states that eCBT is more effective. I asked which estimate is valid, and how robust it is.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

I recomputed each document's headline estimate from the inputs it prints. I re-extracted all trials from the primary reports. A multiverse meta-analysis \cite{simonsohn2020specification, plessen2023multiverse} pooled Hedges' $g$ under every combination of six analytic choices: data source (corrigendum, letter or mine), effect-size metric \cite{becker1988, morris2008}, outcome instrument, included trials, estimator, and the handling of the one trial reporting only change scores \cite{wright2005}. Positive $g$ favours face-to-face CBT; the corrigendum and the letter print the opposite sign.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The corrigendum's Table 1 confirms the letter's diagnosis \cite{kriston2022concerns}: in \nArmsMatch{} of \nArms{} trial arms, the value entered as the SD is the SE of the mean change (Figure~\ref{fig:main}A). This inflates each effect in proportion to the square root of the sample size. With SEs entered as SDs, I reproduce the headline estimates of the original, addendum and corrigendum; the letter's reproduces from its supplement. All four are reproducible, but only the letter's rests on SDs. With SDs, the corrigendum's own table gives $g = \tOneDlEst$ [$\tOneDlLb$, $\tOneDlUb$].

Two findings go beyond the letter. First, the error also inflated heterogeneity, for which the original downgraded its evidence: $I^2$ is $\tOneDlItwo\%$ with SDs, not $\repOrigItwo\%$, and $\noMgItwo\%$ without the two small MoodGYM trials. Second, the corrigendum introduced an error: for Wright 2005 \cite{wright2005}, which reports only change scores, it replaced a correctly derived post-treatment mean with the week-4 change score.

Across \nSpecs{} specifications (Figure~\ref{fig:main}B), $g$ ranges from $\mvMin$ to $\mvMax$ (median $\mvMedian$). The confidence interval includes zero in $\pctCiZero\%$ of them; the \nCiExclFtf{} exceptions favour face-to-face CBT, and none favours eCBT. The three data sources agree: their medians differ by at most $\dsMaxShift$. Trial selection matters most: without the MoodGYM trials, the median falls to $\noMgMedian$. No specification comes within 0.5 of the corrigendum's estimate.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

All four headline estimates are reproducible, but only the letter's is valid: the other three arise only when SEs are entered as SDs. Across three extractions and \nSpecs{} specifications, corrected effects are small and far from the published values. The reanalysis undercuts the target's conclusion that eCBT is more effective, and the corrigendum's claim of unchanged conclusions; it corroborates the letter. The evidence is compatible with small differences in either direction, and the slight advantage for face-to-face CBT rests on two small MoodGYM trials. Neither the addendum nor the corrigendum removed the error; it became checkable only because the corrigendum disclosed study-level data.

\paragraph{Reproducibility}
I reproduced the headline estimate of all four documents, three exactly and one to within rounding; one secondary analysis in the addendum could not be reproduced. \emph{Original} \cite{luo2020}: the forest-plot inputs return the printed $\pubOrig$ [$\pubOrigLb$, $\pubOrigUb$] and $I^2 = \repOrigItwo\%$ exactly, with DerSimonian-Laird pooling and RevMan's variance formula. Because those inputs are standard errors, the result is not a standardised mean difference. \emph{Addendum} \cite{luo2020addendum}: it rereads the same $\pubOrig$. Its $\pubAddm$ [$\pubAddmLb$, $\pubAddmUb$] without Choi 2014 reproduces to within rounding, with the same caveat. Its post-intervention analysis, $0.21$ [$-0.05$, $0.46$], could not be reproduced: the corrigendum's table gives $0.10$ [$-0.13$, $0.33$] (DerSimonian-Laird, RevMan variance), and the pre-correction values in the corrigendum's footnotes give $0.14$ [$-0.13$, $0.40$], or $0.19$ [$-0.09$, $0.46$] without Choi 2014. \emph{Corrigendum} \cite{sanger2021corrigendum}: its forest-plot values return $\pubCorr$ [$\pubCorrLb$, $\pubCorrUb$] and $I^2 = \repCorrItwo\%$ exactly. The computation is correct, but the inputs are standard errors. They also disagree with the corrigendum's own Table 1 in four cells, and for Wright 2005 they rest on week-4 completer change scores, where the original had correctly used baseline minus the endpoint change. \emph{Letter of concern} \cite{kriston2022concerns}: its supplementary table returns the printed $\krPub$ [$\krPubLb$, $\krPubUb$], $Q = \krRepQ$, $I^2 = \krRepItwo\%$ exactly, with DerSimonian-Laird pooling, a change-score SMD at $r = 0.3$ and RevMan's small-sample correction. All 14 per-study values match to two decimals, and the pooled estimate is stable under REML (Hartung-Knapp interval $\krHkLb$ to $\krHkUb$, positive favouring face-to-face CBT). On the same scale, my own extraction gives $\mineKr$ [$\mineKrLb$, $\mineKrUb$] under the letter's specification and $\minePost$ [$\minePostLb$, $\minePostUb$] as a post-test SMD. I found no reply by the original authors to the letter.
